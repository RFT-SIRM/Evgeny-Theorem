PROOF AND VERIFICATION PATH

From a trace identity to reproducible evidence

The six stages below summarize how the H⁴ identity was formulated, computed at finite levels, proved in Lean, and independently checked. They describe this theorem's evidence trail, not a universal pipeline for every result in the repository.

THE MATHEMATICAL OBJECT

A Sierpiński-gasket graph with an SU(2) connection

At refinement level m, the standard Sierpiński-gasket graph carries an SU(2)-valued edge connection. The two configurations below share the graph and flux placement but differ in their rotation-axis pattern.

GRAPH SG(m)

Build the graph by recursive triangle subdivision. Its vertex, edge, and face counts are given exactly below and checked by tests/test_levels.py. One designated edge per elementary triangle carries flux. Each oriented edge (a,b) has a unitary U(a,b) ∈ SU(2), with U(b,a) = U(a,b)†.

|Vm| = (3m+1+3)/2vertices
|Em| = 3m+1edges
|Fm| = 3melementary faces
ℋm = ℂ|Vm| ⊗ ℂ²  ·  dim ℋm = 3m+1 + 3

C Non-commuting connection

The rotation axis on each designated flux edge cycles through x, y, z by triangle index modulo 3. The recorded test gives a commutator norm of 1.0 for the selected pair of edge matrices, confirming that this pair does not commute; no global maximality claim is made.

axis(t) ∈ {x, y, z},   t mod 3

C′ Commuting control

The comparison configuration keeps the graph and flux placement fixed but uses the z axis on every triangle. Its edge matrices commute and split into two decoupled U(1)-magnetic Sierpiński-gasket Laplacians with fluxes ±θ/2.

axis(t) = z for every t  ·  C′ ≅ U(1) ⊕ U(1)

θ = 0 Reduction check

At θ = 0 both configurations reduce to two decoupled copies of the plain Sierpiński-gasket Laplacian. This reduction is covered by a dedicated test.

C(0) = C′(0) = SG ⊕ SG
THE PRINCIPAL THEOREM

An exact H⁴ trace-defect identity

LEAN-KERNEL THEOREM · H⁴

PROJECT-DEFINED DIAGNOSTICS

Six checks for the normalized H⁴ invariant

This is the project's six-criterion diagnostic protocol for the normalized trace defect Im. It is not an external peer-review decision, and the numerical checks are labelled as numerical evidence.

CRITERION I ✓

Exact stable limit

At θ = π/2, Im tends to −8/9. The exact error is −16/[9(3m+1)], so the successive-error ratio tends to 1/3.

CRITERION II ✓

Distinguishes the two connections

C and C′ use the same graph and flux placement but different rotation-axis patterns. The trace defect measures that connection change.

CRITERION III ✓

Survives normalization

The per-state quantity has the finite limit I∞(θ) = −16/9 · sin²(θ/2), nonzero whenever sin(θ/2) ≠ 0.

CRITERION IV ✓

Gauge-invariance check

A numerical vertex-wise SU(2) gauge test at level 3 gives a maximum spectral discrepancy of 7.99×10⁻¹⁵; the recorded M4 difference is 0.

CRITERION V ✓

Not a graph-count effect

The two configurations have identical graph, dimension, edge and face counts, and flux placement. The changed data are the connection axes.

CRITERION VI ✓

First nonzero trace defect at p = 4

The recorded lower-moment calculation gives zero trace defect for p = 1, 2, 3; p = 4 is the first nonzero moment.

SCOPE OF THE H⁴ IDENTITY

What this theorem establishes — and what it does not

✓ Established by the H⁴ theorem

  • An exact formula for the raw fourth-moment trace defect Δm(H⁴,θ), for every integer m ≥ 1 and every real θ; the theorem is checked by the Lean kernel.
  • The normalized limit I∞(θ) = −16/9 · sin²(θ/2), including I∞(π/2) = −8/9.
  • The scalar formula can be evaluated without constructing the finite operator matrix or diagonalizing it. No bit-complexity bound is claimed.
  • The theorem statement, Lean declaration, numerical checks, and reproducibility commands are available in the repository.

◇ Not implied by the H⁴ theorem

  • The identity does not determine the full spectrum, individual eigenvalues, or the spectral gap. Those require separate spectral analysis.
  • This is not a theorem in computational complexity and does not establish an asymptotic algorithm for computing the full spectrum or spectral gap.
  • H⁶, H⁸, and H¹⁰ are separate higher-moment results with their own proof assumptions and formalization status; they are summarized in the next section.

✓ Established results

  • H⁶ (sixth moment). Lean proves the all-angle recurrence for m ≥ 3. An exact symbolic certificate supplies the finite m = 2 → 3 transition, giving the combined all-angle formula for m ≥ 2. That finite bridge is not yet a Lean-kernel theorem.
  • H⁸ (eighth moment). Lean proves the all-angle affine recurrence for m ≥ 3. An exact sparse-polynomial certificate supplies the finite m = 2 → 3 transition, giving the combined formula for m ≥ 2. That finite bridge is not yet a Lean-kernel theorem.
  • H¹⁰ (tenth moment). H10TransportAll_proved proves radius-four diagonal transport through degree nine for both configurations at every m ≥ 4. Lean consequently proves the affine recurrence for m ≥ 4 and the affine formula from level five for m ≥ 5, for every real θ.

◇ Remaining formalization and simplification

  • H⁶ and H⁸ base bridges. Formalize the exact m = 2 → 3 certificates inside Lean if the goal is a fully kernel-checked all-level formula beginning at m = 2. Until then, the certificate is exact but external to the Lean kernel.
